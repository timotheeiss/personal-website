### Newgrain product philosophy

Tim designed Newgrain to feel closer to an art gallery than a conventional social feed:

- Photography should be the centre of attention.
- The interface should feel calm, minimal, intentional, and distraction-free.
- The product should avoid unnecessary popularity games and addictive patterns.
- People should leave inspired rather than drained by comparison or endless scrolling.
- Analog craft should be visible through structured details such as film stock, camera, lens, and development process.
- The core community should remain accessible for free, with monetization attached to additional tools rather than gatekeeping participation.

### Newgrain technical work

- Native iOS app in Swift.
- Web application in React and TypeScript.
- Firebase for authentication, database, and cloud functions.
- AWS S3, Lambda, and CloudFront for image storage, processing, compression, and delivery.
- Algolia for search.
- A masonry-style photo grid designed to preserve image aspect ratios.

Tim made pragmatic tradeoffs. Native iOS produced a strong mobile experience but excluded Android users, motivating the web platform. Firebase enabled fast product development but required care around denormalized records. The original single-page web architecture created a polished application experience but limited search-engine discovery.

### Newgrain growth

Newgrain grew through Reddit, Instagram curation, word of mouth, photography press, and a distinctive product identity. One product-led loop let members add their Instagram usernames during onboarding; Newgrain's curator account featured strong work and tagged photographers, giving them a reason to reshare it with their own audiences. Tim eventually automated parts of this loop, bringing in organic users without paid acquisition.

Newgrain is Tim's strongest evidence of founder-level ownership, product taste, persistence, user empathy, community building, and the ability to ship across design, mobile, web, backend, growth, and operations.

### Research project: agent-testable graphical interfaces

Tim's MSc research project is titled “Generating Agent-Testable GUIs.” It is being conducted with CodeSpeak and Andrey Breslav, the creator of Kotlin.

The research asks how generated web interfaces can become faster, cheaper, and more reliable for AI browsing agents to test. Current browser agents often consume expensive screenshots or large representations of the page and can struggle with navigation controls, nested interfaces, and timing. Tim is exploring the injection of structured hints into generated interfaces so agents can understand page context and capabilities without repeatedly processing the full page.

The work sits at the intersection of:

- AI agents.
- Browser automation.
- Front-end and end-to-end testing.
- Code generation.
- Developer tools.
- Product quality and reliability.

Success is evaluated through time, token consumption, and accuracy when agents execute test scenarios. Early results show 30% lower token use than conventional browser agents. The project is still in development, so this should be described as an early result rather than a final performance claim.
